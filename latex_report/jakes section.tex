\section*{3.3 Leverage-augmented and restricted VAR}

This extension asks whether commodity variance histories add forecast information
after accounting for negative equity returns. We compare a plain VAR with a
\emph{leverage-augmented VAR} (LVAR), and then restrict the commodity lag structure
to assess whether a smaller model retains useful information. The relevant
benchmark for commodity additions is an equity-only model with the same variance
lags and downside controls. This separates the contribution of equity-return
information from that of additional markets.

Let $z_t$ contain ES log RV5 and the included commodity log variances. Our LVAR is
the linear VAR with predetermined explanatory variables (VAR-X)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jake-lvar}
 z_{t+1}=c+\sum_{j=1}^{p} A_jz_{t+1-j}+\Gamma d_t+u_{t+1},
 \qquad p=5.
\end{equation}
Each equation is estimated by OLS. The plain VAR sets $\Gamma=0$; the equity-only
versions are the corresponding AR and AR-X benchmarks. Neither Treasury proxies
nor calendar indicators enter this extension, distinguishing it from the
seasonally controlled VAR in Section 3.2. The term ``leverage'' refers here to
the predictive association of negative equity returns with variance; it does not
identify a particular economic mechanism or a regime-switching process.

Motivated by persistent leverage effects \citep{corsi-reno}, the three controls
are defined for decimal ES close-to-close log returns $r_t$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jake-downside}
 d_t=(d_t^{(1)},d_t^{(5)},d_t^{(22)})',\qquad
 d_t^{(h)}=\left|\min\left(\frac{1}{h}\sum_{i=0}^{h-1}r_{t-i},0\right)\right|.
\end{equation}
The stored percentage returns are divided by 100. Importantly, the negative part
is taken \emph{after averaging signed returns}; the weekly and monthly terms are
not averages of daily negative parts. These controls enter every system equation
and are available at the forecast origin. The specification is our application
of a multihorizon downside-control idea, rather than a replication of the full
LHAR-CJ model in \citet{corsi-reno}.

The \emph{restricted LVAR} retains all five ES variance lags, the three downside
controls and the intercept, but retains only the most recent variance observation
for each commodity. Formally, every entry in a commodity column of $A_j$ is set to
zero for $j=2,\ldots,5$, in every equation. These are predetermined lag-coefficient
restrictions, a special case of constrained or subset VAR estimation
\citep[Chapter 5]{lutkepohl}; manual zero restrictions with equation-by-equation
OLS are also described in the official \texttt{vars} documentation \citep{vars}.
The particular daily-only restriction is a project comparison, not a restriction
prescribed by those sources. It does not impose contemporaneous restrictions to
identify structural shocks. With all four commodities, it reduces the number of
ES-equation coefficients from 29 to 13; without corn, from 24 to 12.

The final panel contains 3,910 observations from 4 January 2011 to 31 August 2026.
Shared HAR/LHAR warm-ups leave 3,867 regression targets, including 3,084 initial
training targets through 12 July 2023. BIC on the initial five-market \emph{plain}
VAR selects five lags from candidates 1--20, using identical training outcomes
for every candidate; AIC selects ten. The five-lag order is held fixed across the
main plain, leverage and restricted comparisons. The 1/5/22 return windows are
fixed features, not separately selected VAR orders. Fits expand chronologically
to produce 783 forecasts for the next retained observation, from 13 July 2023
to 31 August 2026. No target-date commodity values or returns enter the forecast.

Adding downside controls to the equity-only VAR reduces forecast MSE by
9.31\%. Adding oil and gold to the resulting equity-only LVAR instead increases
MSE by 0.72\%; adding all four commodities increases it by 0.14\%. Natural gas
alone lowers MSE by 0.46\%. Descriptive paired circular-block intervals for these
commodity comparisons include zero, whereas the downside-control improvement
remains positive across the examined block lengths. The largest clearly observed
gain in this extension therefore comes from equity-return information rather
than a broad commodity history.

The daily-only restriction lowers the all-four LVAR's MSE by 0.32\%, while using
16 fewer ES-equation coefficients. Its MSE is only 0.18\% below the equity-only
LVAR. The restricted no-corn result is similarly close to that benchmark. These
small descriptive differences support considering a parsimonious specification;
they do not establish statistically significant forecast superiority or show that
omitted commodity lags contain no information. In-sample joint HAC tests still
find conditional commodity associations, illustrating why historical fit and
incremental forecast performance must be distinguished.

ADF rejects a unit root for all five training log-RV5 series, while KPSS rejects
level stationarity. We retain log-variance levels but treat this disagreement as
an unresolved specification limitation. Conditional companion eigenvalues are
below one in the reported training fits, yet ES residual dependence remains.
OLS inference uses HAC covariance with bandwidth 22 \citep{newey-west}; this does
not resolve nonstationarity or omitted dynamics. Ten-lag sensitivity lowers
equity-only LVAR MSE to 0.328510, with oil and gold still failing to improve its
matched benchmark. Forecast rankings and uncertainty intervals remain
exploratory because the evaluation period has already been inspected. We make no
claim of structural causality or tested gains in portfolio utility or Value-at-Risk.

\section*{Integration notes: align the group report before submission}
\reportnote{These notes are for the authors and should be removed from the final

\path{reports/final_dataset/GROUP_REPORT_REVIEW.md}. Teammates' code and the

\textbf{Stationarity and model checks.} ADF/KPSS appropriately test the individual
data series before estimation. They involve their own auxiliary calculations;
they are not tests of a fitted VAR. Fitted companion eigenvalues, residual
autocorrelation and ARCH checks are complementary. No fitted model can be used
to certify away conflicting series-level stationarity evidence.

\textbf{Lag conventions.} The implementation choices are:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{3.1cm}X@{}}\toprule
Model or check & Current rule\\\midrule
Jake VAR/LVAR & Training-only five-market BIC: 5 lags; AIC: 10. Fixed across main comparisons.\\
Restricted LVAR & Same five ES lags; commodity lags 2--5 fixed to zero in every equation.\\
Tim VAR & Training-only six-variable system plus month/weekday controls: BIC 4, AIC 9.\\
HAR/LHAR & Fixed means over 1, 5 and 22 observations, each entering as one regressor.\\
ADF/KPSS & Jake: AIC autolag/automatic bandwidth; Tim: fixed 20/28. These are test settings.\\
HAC(22) & Covariance bandwidth, not a VAR order.\\\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\textbf{Dates and data.} The final CSV starts on 4 January 2011, not 5 January.
It has complete corn data; corn enters the updated Tim and Jake VAR extensions.
ES close returns are used in LHAR and LVAR. The supplied yield proxies describe
changes between retained yield observations, rather than necessarily consecutive
native Treasury dates. The baseline models do not all impose an extra Treasury
availability delay; Tim reports that as a sensitivity. Avoid a blanket delayed-yield
claim in the shared data section.

\textbf{Comparable losses.} Tim and Jake score the same 783 outcomes, but Tim's
initial training history has 3,127 raw rows, while Jake's common warm-ups leave
3,084 training targets. Original HAR uses a separate 80/20 split with 778 test
targets beginning 20 July 2023; LHAR has 774 beginning 26 July. Their printed
scores must not be presented as the same-date comparison. Current original
baseline MSEs are 0.3639 and 0.3254, respectively; the older group PDF shows
0.3592 and 0.3213 and needs its HAR table refreshed.

\textbf{Interpretation and structure.} Replace the report's mean-of-daily-negative
return formula with Equation~\eqref{eq:jake-downside}. Distinguish the downside
gain from incremental commodity value and from comparisons across differently
controlled models. A percent change in log-variance MSE does not imply the same
percent change in VaR. Repair the contents bookmarks, remove duplicated economic
interpretation and comments, complete the empty results/conclusion sections, and
attach the referenced appendices. Tim's current BIC results round to AR MSE
0.356690 and an all-commodity MSE increase of 0.64\%.

\textbf{Figure priorities.} Use the incremental-gain chart in the main text if
